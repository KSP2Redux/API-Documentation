    Struct ThermalAutoShutdownData

    Per-part thermal auto-shutdown state for a module that generates its own heat, such as a mine, a resource converter or a generator.

    Namespace: Redux.Ecs.Components
    Assembly: Assembly-CSharp.dll
    Syntax
    public struct ThermalAutoShutdownData
    Remarks

    The shutdown decision used to live in the view-side ThermalUpdate of each module, which runs once per rendered frame and reads a temperature the flux integration has already committed. That works only while a single integration step is small compared to the gap between the shutdown temperature and the part's maximum. At high timewarp the step is not: one tick advances universe time by the physics timestep multiplied by the warp rate, so at a million times a drill rose by nearly five thousand kelvin in a single step and was destroyed before the shutdown had any intermediate temperature to react to.

    Holding the state here lets the decision run in ThermalAutoShutdownSystem, at the same cadence as the integration and with the step length in hand.

    The request half is written by the sim-side PartComponentModule_*, through RunRequested, FluxGenerated, AutoShutdownEnabled and ShutdownTemperature. That side has to be sim side: ThermalSimGroup is ordered after SimLegacyUpdate, which drives those modules, so a value written there reaches the shutdown on the same tick. The view modules run later in the frame from GameInstance.Update, so publishing from their ThermalUpdate handed the shutdown the previous frame's request and a module that had already stopped still got a full step of heat.

    The result half, HasOverheated and AppliedFlux, is read back by the view modules for their status and readouts. That direction is same-frame and can stay in the view.

    This is one component per part, and each publishing module overwrites the whole struct, so it assumes at most one heat-generating module per part. Two of them would silently stomp each other, which is what the ThermalData.OtherFlux field they replaced already did. No stock part carries two. The one case that comes close is an engine alternator, a Module_Generator sharing a part with a Module_Engine, and every one of those is authored with a FluxGenerated of zero, which Resolve(ref ThermalAutoShutdownData, ref ThermalData, double, double) skips before it touches the flux field so the engine keeps its own heat.

    A mod is the likely way the assumption gets broken. A single part that both mines and converts what it mines is a reasonable thing for someone to author, and nothing stops a mod from putting a Module_Mine and a Module_ResourceConverter on the same part. That is the point at which this code has to be updated, and the symptom would be one of the two modules generating no heat at all rather than anything failing loudly.

    If a part ever needs two, the established pattern to follow is MassModifierData: a component on each module entity, reduced into the part by a system, the way MassAggregateSystem reduces into PartMassModifier. Note that mass reduces by summation and this does not. Summing FluxGenerated is the easy half, and how two modules with different shutdown temperatures and separate latches should divide one part's headroom is an open question that wants answering against a real part rather than in advance.

    Remarks

    Only a module that resumes on its own needs a band. The mine and the converter drop their enable toggle when they trip, so the player restarts them and there is nothing to cycle. The generator deliberately keeps its toggle, since SetGeneratorState is written to let it come back once cool, so without a band it would clear the instant it touched its threshold, resume at full output, reach the threshold again in the same step and trip once more, flicking its status, its audio cue and its resource output on and off tick by tick.

    Left at zero this falls back to ShutdownTemperature, which is the no-band behaviour and stops an unset field from holding a part overheated forever.

    Remarks

    The step bound applies to the module's own heat only, so the promise is that a module cannot cook its own part, not that the part survives. Heat arriving from anywhere else is caught reactively instead: the temperature latch below shuts the module down on the first step that finds the part over its threshold, whatever drove it there.

    Folding the other sources into the headroom is deliberately not done yet. The environment and reentry fluxes on ThermalData are computed by IntegrateFluxJob, which runs after this system, so the values visible here are a step old. More to the point they are not currently trustworthy at the warp rates this bound exists for. The negative branch of that job applies an unbounded step of its own, which drives a landed part below absolute zero and clamps it, and a drill on Laythe was measured alternating between 0 K and 482.758 K on consecutive ticks at 100000x with the environment flux flipping between 0 and -5905.305. Feeding that into the projection would make the applied flux flap for no physical reason. Widen this once the integration's own steps are bounded.

    Static and free of ECS state so the whole decision, ownership of the flux field included, can be tested without a world, a GameManager or loaded physics settings.

    ResolveAppliedFlux(double, double, double, double, double, double, out bool)

    double shutdownTemperature

    Temperature the module shuts itself down at.
    double thermalMass

    The part's thermal mass.
    double deltaUniversalTime

    Length of the integration step in universe seconds.
    double temperatureRiseScale

    Product of the difficulty heat scale and the global positive temperature change rate scale, so the projection matches what the flux integration will do with the returned flux.
    bool hasTripped

    True when the step reaches the shutdown temperature.
    Returns
    Type Description
    double

    The flux to apply over this step.
    Remarks

    A step long enough to carry the part past shutdownTemperature is treated as the module running for only the part of the interval that gets it there and being shut down for the rest, so the averaged flux over the whole step lands the part on the threshold rather than far beyond it. That is what the once-per-frame check could not express: it could only choose between the full flux and none, and at high timewarp the full flux for one step was already past the part's maximum temperature.

    The bound covers the module's own contribution only. The integration sums reentry, environment and exhaust flux into the same step, and none of those are subtracted from the headroom here, so a module running while the part is being heated from elsewhere can still be carried past its threshold and on past its maximum. See Resolve(ref ThermalAutoShutdownData, ref ThermalData, double, double) for why the other sources are not folded in and what catches that case instead.

    Static and free of ECS state so the decision can be tested directly.
