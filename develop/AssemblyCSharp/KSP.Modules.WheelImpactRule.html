    Class WheelImpactRule

    Physical damage criteria for a wheel or landing leg, from the solver's outputs. Damage is energy based: the work the bump stop absorbs beyond the design travel plus the kinetic energy of a touchdown, decaying between hits, against a capacity derived from the rated load and the travel. A load alone, however high for one step, never breaks a wheel. A sustained structural overload does.

    Remarks

    Stock parts carry no damage module, so before the revamp a wheel "broke" when the native suspension bottomed out and the part's own colliders hit the terrain above its crash tolerance. The bump stop now keeps the part off the ground, so the same physics is assessed here from the contact instead.

    ENERGY_CAPACITY_FACTOR

    Capacity factor for free rolling wheels, the aircraft gear: the part absorbs this many times its rated load times its full travel before it breaks. A single factor cannot serve every part, because the factor is the construction margin of the part itself. Aircraft gear is built to survive hard landings and carries it at 20: the jet nose gear (rated 8 t, 0.18 m of travel and tire) then breaks at about 17 m/s, past the 12 m/s aircraft certification drop test speed, while a full lock taxi turn at 20 m/s on landinggear_0v still reaches only 0.002 with the stop engaged, so cornering load transfer never accumulates. Driven rovers use ROVER_ENERGY_CAPACITY_FACTOR and landing legs LEG_ENERGY_CAPACITY_FACTOR.

    ROVER_ENERGY_CAPACITY_FACTOR

    Capacity factor for driven rover wheels, which run on soft ground and take the bumps the suspension does not. At 6 the wheel_0v_rover (rated 8 t, 0.195 m plus tire) breaks at about 42 m/s, a ninety three metre drop, far past any surface landing a rover takes, while a six metre drop stays under a tenth of the capacity.
